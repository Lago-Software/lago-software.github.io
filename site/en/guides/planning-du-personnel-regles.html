Staff scheduling in France: the rules to follow in 2026

In a leisure venue or shop, the schedule changes often: school holidays, the Saturday rush, last-minute absences. Here are the French Labour Code rules to keep in mind before filling in the boxes.

Published on 13 November 2026 · by the Lago team

Maximum working hours

  • 10 hours of actual work per day at most. A collective agreement can go up to 12 hours in some cases.
  • 48 hours in any one week at most.
  • 44 hours per week on average over 12 consecutive weeks. A collective agreement or a labour inspectorate authorisation can raise this average to 46 hours.

Rest periods

  • Daily rest: 11 consecutive hours between two working days, unless exemptions apply. Watch out for late closures followed by an opening the next morning.
  • Weekly rest: a full 24-hour day, plus the 11 hours of daily rest, i.e. 35 consecutive hours.
  • Break: at least 20 consecutive minutes once working time reaches 6 hours.

Part-time staff: give notice in time

For a part-time employee, any change to how their hours are spread over the days of the week or the weeks of the month must be notified with a notice period. Without a collective agreement, this period is 7 working days; an agreement can shorten it, but not below 3 working days.

Employees under 18

Summer and weekend jobs often attract minors. More protective rules apply to them: shorter hours, longer breaks and rest. Check them before putting them on a late closing shift.

Your collective agreement matters

Your collective agreement may set specific rules: deadlines for publishing the schedule, daily span, Sunday work, pay supplements. Re-read it, or ask your accountant or payroll provider.

Good habits

  • Start from a standard week, then create special periods for the holidays.
  • Set a minimum staffing level per time band, and spot the gaps before they happen.
  • Route leave requests through a single channel, with clear approval.
  • Set rules for shift swaps between colleagues: who approves, and on what terms?
  • Keep your schedules: in a dispute over hours or rest, it is often up to the employer to provide the proof.
