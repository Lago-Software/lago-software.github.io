Children’s birthday parties: organising online booking at a leisure venue

Birthday parties are often very profitable bookings, and also the most time-consuming: calls, quotes, deposits, last-minute changes. Here is how to organise them so they almost run themselves.

Published on 14 November 2026 · by the Lago team

Clear packages

Offer two or three packages at most, with everything included: length of the activity, snack, drinks, room, host, invitations, a gift for the child. State the minimum number of children and the price per extra child. The fewer questions, the fewer calls.

Dedicated time slots

A birthday party takes up both a room and places within the activity’s capacity. Plan specific time slots, for example Wednesday afternoons and weekends, and make sure a booking automatically blocks both. Otherwise the room is free but the park is full, or the other way round.

The online deposit

Asking for a deposit when booking greatly reduces no-shows. Online payment also saves chasing people. Put your cancellation and postponement terms in writing: in France and the EU, the 14-day right of withdrawal does not apply to a leisure activity on a specific date, but your terms must be clear and accepted at the time of booking.

Quotes for groups

Schools, holiday clubs, works councils: groups often ask for a quote before booking. A fast quote with a deposit payment link turns into a booking far more often than a quote sent three days later.

On the day

  • prepare the guest list and the confirmed number of children;
  • have the rules and waivers signed in advance, on a tablet, rather than at reception at 2 pm on a Saturday (see our guide on waivers);
  • give the group wristbands of the same colour;
  • take the balance and any extras on the same ticket.

After the party

Every birthday party brings around ten families who didn’t always know your venue. Offer a gift card, a loyalty programme or a deal for their next visit: it’s the best moment to bring them back.
